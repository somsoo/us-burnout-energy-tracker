      🧮 How the scores work

      • The questions: the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory, a free, public-domain questionnaire from Danish researchers (Kristensen and colleagues, 2005). It measures physical and psychological fatigue and exhaustion in three areas: personal (6 questions), work-related (7) and client-related (6, only if your work involves people you serve).
      • Scoring: each answer scores 100 (Always or To a very high degree), 75, 50, 25 or 0 (Never or almost never, or To a very low degree). The question about energy for family and friends is scored the other way round, because more energy means less burnout. Each area's score is the average of its answers, from 0 to 100.
      • Overall score and battery: the overall score is the average of all your answers. The mental battery is 100 minus that, so a full battery means no signs of burnout.
      • The 50 line: research studies commonly treat an average of 50 or more as high burnout. The meter flags any area at 50 or above. It is a screening line, not a diagnosis.
      • History: Save to my history keeps the date and your scores in this browser, one check-in per day, so you can see your trend.

      Sources: Kristensen and colleagues, The Copenhagen Burnout Inventory, Work & Stress, 2005 (questions and scoring as listed by the UC San Diego EMERGE measures library and in a 2022 American Board of Emergency Medicine study in JACEP Open); Li and colleagues, Academic Emergency Medicine, 2024 (the cut-off of 50); World Health Organization, ICD-11 definition of burn-out; 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.       ❓ Frequently asked questions

      What is the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory?

      A free, public-domain burnout questionnaire published by Danish researchers in 2005 (Kristensen and colleagues). It asks how often and how strongly you feel tired and worn out, with separate scores for personal, work-related and client-related burnout. It has been tested in many countries and jobs, including doctors, nurses, pharmacists, teachers and students.

      What does my score mean?

      Each area is scored from 0 to 100. A score of 50 means your average answer was “sometimes” or “somewhat”. Research studies commonly treat 50 or more as high burnout. Your mental battery is 100 minus your overall score, so a lower battery means more exhaustion.

      Do I have to answer the client questions?

      Only if your work involves people you serve, such as clients, patients, customers or students. Tick the box to add those six questions and choose the word that fits your job. Your personal and work-related scores do not depend on them.

      Is this a diagnosis?

      No. It is a self-check, not a medical or psychological assessment. The World Health Organization describes burnout as an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition. Long-lasting exhaustion can also come from sleep problems, depression or other health issues, so talk with a doctor if it lasts or gets in the way of daily life. In a crisis, call or text 988, or call 911 in an emergency.

      Where are my answers saved?

      Nowhere unless you choose. Your answers are not sent to us. Your scores are saved in this browser only when you press Save to my history, and Clear history deletes them. If you press Copy result or Share link, your answers are put into the link so the person who opens it sees the same result.
